Florida Turnpike Expansion: What St. Andrews Owners Need to Know

By Edmund Bogen · Douglas Elliman Real Estate · August 7, 2026
This article was written with the assistance of artificial intelligence.

The Florida Turnpike widening that borders St. Andrews Country Club is no longer a rumor. It is designed, funded, and scheduled: construction begins in the third quarter of 2027, and the roadway between Glades Road and the L-38 Canal grows from six lanes to eight, plus an auxiliary lane in each direction.

Here is what most owners have not heard: FDOT's own draft analysis recommends more than a mile of new noise walls along St. Andrews — and the best available research says that for the homes closest to those walls, sound barriers have historically supported property values, not hurt them.

What Exactly Is Being Built

The controlling project is FDOT Project 417132-1, covering roughly 3.4 miles of the Turnpike from north of Glades Road to north of the L-38 Canal — the stretch that runs directly along St. Andrews' western boundary between Clint Moore Road and the canal.

The design includes four general-use lanes in each direction (up from three), one auxiliary lane in each direction, replacement of the Yamato Road and Clint Moore Road bridges, a realigned southbound Glades Road exit ramp, and new noise walls where FDOT's criteria are met. Roughly $304.5 million in construction funding is programmed for fiscal year 2027 — the highest-certainty tier in the state's work program.

Three facts matter for owners, and all three are favorable:

No land is being taken. The widening fits within the existing Turnpike right-of-way. Programmed right-of-way acquisition for the entire 3.4-mile segment is under $250,000 — a rounding error. No St. Andrews homes and no golf-course land are identified for acquisition.

No new tolls. The added lanes are general-purpose lanes, not tolled express lanes.

The noise walls are recommended, not speculative. FDOT's 2026 draft noise study analyzed 152 St. Andrews residences plus golf-course locations and recommends approximately 5,675 feet — about 1.1 miles — of barriers along the community, most of it 14 feet high, at a cost of roughly $2.69 million. The study projects an average noise reduction of about 7.5 decibels for the homes that benefit.

The Timeline

Design finishes at the end of 2026. Construction on the St. Andrews segment begins in the third quarter of 2027 and is expected to run approximately five years — call it 2032. The segments to the north (L-38 to Atlantic Avenue, then Atlantic to Boynton Beach Boulevard) follow in fiscal 2029 and 2031, so corridor-wide work continues into the early 2030s even after the St. Andrews stretch is done.

What This Means for Property Values

This is the question every owner should be asking, and the honest answer is: it depends on where your home sits — and on the difference between the construction years and what comes after.

Interior homes — the vast majority of St. Andrews — should see little direct effect. Academic research consistently finds that highway noise and proximity effects fade to nearly nothing beyond about 400 meters. For most of the community, club economics, renovation quality, and lot characteristics will keep driving prices, exactly as they do today.

For perimeter homes, the walls are the story. A September 2025 National Bureau of Economic Research working paper studied nearly 600,000 Florida properties around newly built highway sound barriers. Homes within 100 meters of a new wall saw prices rise approximately 6.8 percent — a gain that proved immediate and largely permanent — with measurable benefits extending out to 400 meters. The researchers estimated roughly a one-percent value gain per decibel of noise reduction. Apply that lens to a projected 7.5-decibel average reduction behind a 14-foot wall, and the post-completion math for Turnpike-adjacent homes starts to look like an upgrade over today's condition, not a downgrade.

We also have a local precedent. When I-95 was widened through Boca Raton and Delray Beach, the state installed a chain of sound walls along the adjacent neighborhoods. Residents described the before-and-after difference as night and day. That project is the closest real-world analog to what St. Andrews will experience.

The construction window is the trade-off. From late 2027 until the walls and landscaping are complete, expect the usual drag on the most exposed listings: fewer buyers willing to look past visible construction, longer marketing times, more negotiation. Studies of comparable highway projects found values on nearby homes dipped modestly during construction and recovered once the work finished. Owners who can choose their timing should factor that arc into their plans.

The caveat worth respecting: not every St. Andrews perimeter home gets the full benefit. The wall sections at the community's north and south ends are limited to 8 feet because they must sit atop existing retaining structures, and FDOT's own analysis says some homes there will not receive the full five-decibel reduction. The difference between an 8-foot section and a 14-foot section could matter to an individual property. That is a parcel-level question — and it is answerable.

What Smart Owners Do Now

The design is final at the end of this year. Between now and then, the advantage goes to owners who get specific:

1. Know your wall. Which segment sits behind your property line — 14-foot shoulder wall, 8-foot structure-mounted section, or a transition between them? I can help you read the FDOT maps for your exact parcel.

2. Document today's baseline. Perimeter owners should capture current conditions — sound, sightlines — so the before-and-after case is provable when they sell.

3. If you're selling in the next 18 months, the project is now a known, disclosable fact — Florida law requires disclosure of material facts affecting value. The right move is to get ahead of it with accurate information: the wall recommendation, no land takings, no new tolls, and the value-recovery research. Buyers fear the unknown far more than they fear a documented plan with a funded mitigation.

4. If you're buying, the construction window may be the opportunity. The research says the discount, where it appears, has historically been temporary — and the wall is permanent.

The bottom line: this project is coming, it is fully funded, and for St. Andrews the state's own analysis pairs it with the most substantial noise-mitigation investment the community has ever been offered. The owners who win the next five years will be the ones who trade rumor for parcel-level facts.
