A Camp David in Palm Beach County: What Trump's Presidential Retreat Proposal Means for South Florida

The president wants his West Palm Beach golf club to become the permanent presidential retreat. Here is what is actually on the table, what is not, and what it could mean twenty miles south of the fairway.

What is actually being proposed

On September 3, Harvey E. Oyer III, an attorney for Trump International Golf Club, sent Palm Beach County commissioners a letter asking the county to designate the club as the permanent "United States Presidential Golf Course" — a secure retreat available to current and future presidents of either party, and to visiting foreign heads of state. The Palm Beach Post broke the story, and on October 7 President Trump publicly endorsed it, describing the concept as "like sort of a Camp David retreat," according to NBC News and the Associated Press.

You may have heard versions of this story attached to Trump National Doral in Miami. That is a conflation. Doral is hosting the 2026 G20 Summit on December 14–15, which the White House says will be run "at cost" — but the retreat proposal on the table is specifically about the West Palm Beach club, two minutes from the newly renamed President Donald J. Trump International Airport.

Under the proposal, the club — not taxpayers — would pay to build a presidential lodging facility, designed with the General Services Administration and the Secret Service, and would give the federal government use of the facility and club amenities at no charge. No construction cost, site plan, or timeline has been disclosed.

"This is a letter and an endorsement, not a groundbreaking. The land is county-owned, the commission has not voted, and every hard number is still blank."

Why the county holds the cards

Here is the detail most of the national coverage skips: the golf course sits on roughly 214 acres of county-owned land near the airport, held under long-term leases the Trump Organization signed in 1996 and 2002. The contemplated structure would have the county lease or convey the needed parcel to the federal government while remaining the landlord if federal use ever ends. County Administrator Joseph Abruzzo has called Palm Beach County "uniquely positioned for this historic opportunity," but commissioners are still asking the two right questions: who pays, and what happens to airport operations. There has been no vote.

The security question cuts both ways

Trump says the idea came from the military and the Secret Service, citing the club's proximity to the airport, the absence of surrounding high-rises, and security already in place. The counterargument is just as concrete: this is the same course where a gunman established a concealed position along the perimeter fence in September 2024, in an attempted assassination for which Ryan Routh was sentenced in February to life plus seven years, per the Department of Justice. One former FBI agent told WPTV the site is "absolutely impossible to secure." A permanent retreat would mean hardening that perimeter to a standard the property has never had — which is precisely what the proposal's GSA and Secret Service design process would exist to do.

There is also a cost reality worth saying plainly: "the club pays for the building" is not the same as "this costs the public nothing." Palm Beach County approved a $20 million transfer for presidential-protection costs in February 2025, after the Sheriff's Office reported roughly $13 million in overtime and projected $32 million more through September 2025. A permanent, frequently used retreat would raise the same reimbursement questions all over again, unless a federal mechanism is established.

What the Mar-a-Lago precedent tells us

We do not have to guess what a permanent presidential footprint does to a Palm Beach County neighborhood, because we are living through one. The FAA imposed a continuous flight restriction within one mile of Mar-a-Lago below 2,000 feet starting October 20, 2025. County officials say the redirected departure paths moved aircraft noise from about 2,200 homes to roughly 11,000, and the county has spent over $600,000 fighting the restriction in court, per WFLX. That is the cost side, and it lands hyper-locally — on the blocks nearest the protected site and the airport.

The demand side spreads much wider. Palm Beach reporting has documented a surge of high-end buying since late 2024 — Luxury Tribune reported $10 million-plus properties under contract up 400 percent year-over-year in the weeks after the election, with volume up 612 percent to roughly $291 million. I will be straight with you about the caveat: no controlled study has isolated a "Mar-a-Lago effect" from Florida's tax advantages, migration, and thin inventory, which were all pushing the same direction. But the pattern — costs concentrated within a mile or two, prestige and demand radiating across the corridor — is well documented.

"The Mar-a-Lago record shows the burdens land within a mile of the gates. The halo travels the whole corridor."

What this means at St. Andrews

St. Andrews Country Club sits roughly twenty miles south of Trump International — far outside any plausible flight restriction or motorcade route under the Mar-a-Lago precedent, and well clear of the airport-area disruption. So for Boca Raton's club communities, this proposal is nearly all halo and no hassle. A permanent presidential retreat, a renamed international airport, and a G20 summit down the road in Doral would institutionalize what buyers already sense: the Palm Beach–Boca corridor has become a global power address, with the security infrastructure and private-aviation access that the top of the market increasingly treats as an amenity.

Inside the gates this week there are eleven active listings from $2.5 million to $19.975 million, against 26 sales so far this year — including my own new listing at 7080 Lions Head Lane. In my opinion, communities built around gated security, golf, and privacy are exactly the product this moment favors. But none of it is guaranteed: the retreat is a proposal awaiting a county vote, the demand data has competing explanations, and this is market commentary, not investment advice.

Camp David sits in the Maryland woods. Whether or not its Florida counterpart ever breaks ground, the fact that the conversation is happening here tells you where the center of gravity has moved.
