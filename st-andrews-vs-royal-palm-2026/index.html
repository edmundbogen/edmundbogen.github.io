Two Clubs, Two Markets: What 2026 Sales Reveal About St. Andrews and Royal Palm

One club sells the water. The other sells the club. This year's sales show how differently the two markets behave.

Two clubs, two very different models

St. Andrews Country Club and Royal Palm Yacht & Country Club are two of Boca Raton's established club communities, and they are built on opposite ideas. St. Andrews is a mandatory-membership community: buying the home means joining the club. Since September 1, 2026, a new owner contributes $500,000 to the club and $50,000 to the property owners' association at closing. Annual club dues for 2026 are $50,566 plus tax, before property owners' association assessments.

Royal Palm works the other way. Owning a home there does not make you a member, and the club describes membership as by invitation, with no residency requirement and no published prices. Royal Palm Properties, a brokerage that specializes in the community, reports a golf initiation of about $210,000 with annual dues around $30,000, and lists the homeowners' association fee at $4,150 per lot per year. The club does not publish its rates, so treat those membership figures as broker estimates.

What 41 and 27 sales tell us

I pulled every single-family sale recorded in BeachesMLS for both communities from January 1 through October 20, 2026. Royal Palm recorded 41 closings at a median price of $10.25 million. St. Andrews recorded 26 in the MLS; our team counts 27 once a sale that never reached the MLS is included. The St. Andrews median was $5.18 million.

The price gap is the headline most people expect. The speed gap is the one that surprised me. The median St. Andrews home spent 47 days on the market. The median Royal Palm home spent 106. Eight St. Andrews homes went under contract within four days of listing, compared with five at Royal Palm, despite Royal Palm's larger number of sales.

"Royal Palm is the pricier market. St. Andrews is the faster one. Those are two different kinds of strength."

How far from the asking price

Sellers in both clubs gave ground, but not equally. At St. Andrews, 8 of 26 sellers (31 percent) cut their price before going under contract. At Royal Palm it was 18 of 41 (44 percent). The MLS records whether the final asking price was below the original, not how many separate reductions there were, so these are counts of homes, not of cuts.

On average, a St. Andrews home closed at 91.4 percent of its original asking price, about 8.6 percent below the first number on the listing. A Royal Palm home closed at 88.2 percent, or about 11.8 percent below. Homes that had already cut their price sold for 14.0 percent under their original ask at St. Andrews and 16.2 percent under at Royal Palm. One sale skews any Royal Palm average: the $75 million sale of 2500 East Maya Palm Drive on May 20, reported as a Boca Raton home-sale record. Excluding that sale, the Royal Palm average is about $11.25 million.

Gatehouses, streets and the water

Security is often part of this conversation. St. Andrews describes itself as a private, gated community with staffed entrances. Royal Palm's homeowners' association describes two gatehouses, on Federal Highway and East Camino Real, and a 23-member security unit that patrols around the clock. The difference is in the roads. The Coastal Star reported in 2010 that Royal Palm's streets belong to the City of Boca Raton, and the association calls Royal Palm a limited-access community. I could not find a more recent public record on street ownership, so I would describe Royal Palm as guarded rather than gated.

Royal Palm also has something St. Andrews cannot match: ocean access. About 229 of its lots are on the water, the club runs a 60-slip marina, and Royal Palm Properties describes the community as having direct ocean and Intracoastal access. St. Andrews answers with land: 36 holes of golf on more than 700 acres, with about 70 acres of lakes.

What this means at St. Andrews

Inventory tells the rest of the story. Today there are 12 homes for sale at St. Andrews, from $2.5 million to $19.975 million, against 26 at Royal Palm, from about $6.2 million to $38 million. At this year's pace of sales, that is roughly four months of supply at St. Andrews and six at Royal Palm.

In my own practice, I am meeting buyers who began their search in Royal Palm and found the price of entry out of reach. My opinion is that St. Andrews should not be sold to them as "Royal Palm for less." It is a different proposition: a full club that comes with the house, two golf courses, gated entry with staffed entrances and a market where homes sell faster and closer to asking. The honest counterpoint is cost. Mandatory membership adds a meaningful sum at closing and every year after, which narrows the gap for a buyer who would live in Royal Palm without joining its club. And no amount of golf replaces a deep-water dock.

For owners here, this year's data is encouraging: priced right, St. Andrews homes have been moving. Priced too high, homes in both clubs have sat.
