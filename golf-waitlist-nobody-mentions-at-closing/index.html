The Golf Waitlist Nobody Mentions at Closing

Buyers compare kitchens, lots and dues. Fewer ask what they can actually use the day they close. I read our clubs’ membership documents and a year of sales to see how golf access really works.
By Edmund Bogen · November 19, 2026 · Written with the assistance of artificial intelligence — every figure sourced below

When buyers tour club communities with me, they compare kitchens, lot sizes and dues. Very few ask the question that decides how they will spend their first season: what can I actually use the day I close? In Boca Raton and Delray Beach the answer varies more than most people expect, especially when it comes to golf. At some clubs golf comes with every membership; at others it is capped or sits behind a waitlist, and buyers don’t always hear about that before closing. So this week I read the membership documents our clubs publish and pulled a year of sales from BeachesMLS.

One disclosure before I go further: I live at St. Andrews Country Club and I am a member. Read what follows with that in mind. I have tried to describe every club by what its own published documents say, and every one of them has something real to offer.

Why the question matters now

Private golf has rarely been in higher demand. According to Golf Digest, 46 percent of U.S. private clubs now have a waiting list, and the median initiation fee is $56,000. Before the pandemic, Club Benchmarking’s founder told the magazine, the median was $25,000 and about a quarter of clubs had a wait list. The same article notes that waits are generally easier in the South than in the North, and that in Naples the number of clubs with memberships available rose from five to 17 in a year. Access is a real advantage today, but markets move, so it is worth checking club by club rather than assuming.

Four ways our clubs handle golf

Most club communities around Boca and Delray require membership when you buy, which is part of what makes them work. What differs is what that membership includes on day one.

Each model suits a different buyer. If golf is the point, a club where every membership is a golf membership removes the question entirely. If you play now and then, a base membership with golf as an option can mean lower dues. And if you would rather have a resort clubhouse and racquet sports, the newer communities without a course do that beautifully.

The third group deserves a closer look, because it is where buyers are most often surprised. Woodfield Country Club caps its golf, or Full, memberships at 425. Its own FAQ says, “If a Full membership is available, a Standard member may upgrade at any time by paying the difference in equity and dues.” At Addison Reserve the membership category comes with the house. The club’s rate schedule says owners of a home with a partial golf membership “may place their name on the golf waitlist,” and that “The Club offers no assurance as to the time frame for conversion.” Both are sought-after clubs, and that demand is exactly why a buyer should ask before choosing a house.

What waiting can cost

Addison’s published schedule, effective October 1, 2026, shows how this plays out. Annual dues are $33,824 for a partial golf membership and $49,195 for full golf. Partial golf members pay greens fees, $110 for 18 holes from November through April. And “additional monies will be required to change membership categories.” None of that is a criticism; it is simply a cost a buyer should know before choosing a house, because at a club like this the house you buy decides the golf you get.

What the sales data says

I pulled every single-family closing from November 18, 2025 through November 18, 2026 in these communities, each limited to its own ZIP code. St. Andrews homes sold for a median of $978 per square foot, the highest of the club communities I tracked other than the two waterfront enclaves east of Federal Highway, Royal Palm Yacht & Country Club and The Sanctuary.

I want to be careful with that number. Price per square foot reflects location, lot size and the homes themselves, not just the club, and I am not claiming golf access alone explains it. Homes at Woodfield and Addison Reserve also sold quickly, which tells me buyers want golf everywhere. The real question is when they get it.

What this means at St. Andrews

At St. Andrews, golf membership is mandatory, and it is full golf on two 18-hole courses. That means a buyer becomes a golf member at closing and can play right away, with no golf waitlist. Membership is subject to club approval. The current cost to join is $500,000 for the club plus a $50,000 fee for the property owners’ association, with annual dues on top.

St. Andrews residents: join our members-only Instagram community, @standrewsmembers — the stories inside the gates, exclusively for people who live here at St. Andrews and their immediate family.

What this means for you as a buyer

Ask for the current fee schedule and membership rules in writing before you make an offer, not after. Ask whether the membership that comes with the house includes golf, whether golf is capped, and whether there is a list. And if you are selling at a club where golf comes with every membership, say so in the first line of your listing. Buyers coming from waitlist clubs elsewhere will notice.

If you are comparing club communities this season, ask every club the same question: what can I use the day I close? I am glad to walk you through how each one works, whichever you choose.
