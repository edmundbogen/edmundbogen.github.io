Amendment 3 Passed. Here’s What It Means for St. Andrews.

We ran the numbers on a theoretical $3 million home bought here three years ago. Its value keeps climbing, its tax bill grows at less than half the pace, and anyone moving to Florida has until December 31 to lock in the full exemption.
By Edmund Bogen · November 4, 2026 · Written with the assistance of artificial intelligence — every figure sourced below

On Tuesday, Florida voters approved Amendment 3, the property tax measure that has been the talk of every dinner table at the club since summer. It needed 60 percent to pass and, according to unofficial results reported by CBS12 and Bay News 9, it cleared that bar with about 62 percent of the vote. For homeowners at St. Andrews, this is good news. It is not a windfall, and I'll be straight with you about the size of it. But it changes the math of owning here in a way that favors the people who already live here and the buyers who move quickly.

What actually passed

Today, a Florida homestead gets a total exemption of $51,411: $25,000 that applies to every levy, plus a second, inflation-indexed amount that applies to everything except school taxes. According to the Pinellas County Property Appraiser's summary of the amendment, Amendment 3 raises the exemption on non-school levies to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, then indexes it to inflation from 2029 on. The school exemption stays at $25,000.

Three other pieces matter here. Save Our Homes, the cap that limits how much a homestead's assessed value can rise each year, and portability, which lets you carry that savings to your next Florida home, are both unchanged. For non-homestead property, including second homes, the annual cap on assessment increases drops from 10 percent to 5 percent for non-school levies. And the changes take effect January 1, 2027, so you'll first see them on the TRIM notice that arrives in August 2027 (Florida Senate staff analysis; Pinellas County Property Appraiser).

A theoretical $3 million St. Andrews home

To make this concrete, I modeled a home bought here three years ago for $3 million. To keep it simple, assume the owners filed for homestead and the assessed value was set at the full $3 million for 2024. From there, Save Our Homes holds the assessed value to the state's published caps (2.9 percent for 2025 and 2.7 percent for 2026, per the Florida Department of Revenue), so by 2026 it sits at $3,170,349, and I've assumed 3 percent a year after that. The tax rates are the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser's 2026 proposed millage for this area: 10.9653 mills on non-school levies and 6.287 on school levies. These figures exclude non-ad valorem assessments such as solid waste. Treat them as illustrations, not a tax bill.

Here is the line I'd underline. Between 2026 and 2028, this home's assessed value rises about 6 percent. Its tax bill rises about 2 percent. Over the full stretch from 2026 to 2029, the value climbs roughly 9 percent while the bill grows about 5 percent. Your home keeps appreciating, and the cost of holding it grows at less than half the pace.

"Assessed value up about 6 percent. Tax bill up about 2 percent. That's Amendment 3 in one line."

Because the new exemption is a flat dollar amount, the savings are about the same on a $3 million home as on a $10 million one: roughly $2,200 a year at full phase-in under these assumptions. On a large bill that's a modest share. But it's permanent, it grows with inflation after 2029, and it sits on top of the Save Our Homes protection you already have.

The December 31 window: why buyers will move now

This is the part that matters most for anyone thinking about making Florida home. Amendment 3 gives its full exemption only to people who are permanent Florida residents as of December 31, 2026. People who become residents on or after January 1, 2027 start with a smaller $50,000 non-school exemption, keep it for four years, and get the full exemption beginning in year five. That is how the Pinellas and Manatee County Property Appraisers both summarize it, and the Florida Senate staff analysis ties the rule to the same December 31, 2026 date. The wording varies a little from one official summary to the next, so anyone relocating should confirm their own situation with a tax adviser or attorney.

On a home like our example, waiting until January would cost a newcomer roughly $1,100 in 2027 and about $2,200 a year after that, or close to $7,700 over the four-year phase-in (author's estimate at today's rates). That is real money, and it comes with a hard date. Palm Beach Gardens broker Jeff Lichtenstein told WPTV he expects a rush of buyers trying to beat that deadline. I agree. If you have been planning a move to Florida for "sometime next year," this is the reason to make it this year.

Keep in mind that becoming a Florida resident and qualifying a particular home for homestead are two separate steps. The law firm Bilzin Sumberg notes that evidence of residency can include a Florida driver's license, voter registration, vehicle registration and a declaration of domicile. A home you close on before December 31 gives you the place to make that move. With eight weeks left, I think November and December will be busier than a typical holiday season inside the gates.

Seasonal owners win too

If St. Andrews is your second home, the change is quieter but real. Your assessed value can now rise no more than 5 percent a year on non-school levies, down from 10 percent. That doesn't lower today's bill, but it makes future bills far more predictable, which is exactly what seasonal owners have asked for during years of rising values.

What it means for values here

My opinion, clearly labeled as opinion: Amendment 3 is a tailwind for St. Andrews values, not a rocket. You may have seen headlines about Florida home values jumping 7 to 9 percent. That projection, from a 2025 Realtor.com study reported by Action News Jax, modeled eliminating property taxes entirely, which is not what passed. I wouldn't count on that.

What I do expect is steadier support. Lower holding costs for full-time residents make it easier to stay, and Save Our Homes already gives long-time owners a strong reason not to move. Fewer owners selling means inventory inside the gates stays tight, and tight inventory supports prices. Add the December 31 urgency and a 5 percent cap that makes second-home ownership more predictable, and the incentives point the same direction. Florida Realtors supported the amendment.

The fair counterpoint

Not everyone is cheering. Palm Beach County Administrator Joe Abruzzo told CBS12 the county stands to lose nearly $250 million a year, adding, "This is not a tax decrease; it's simply a tax shift." Property Appraiser Dorothy Jacks called it "a tax reduction… not an elimination." Florida TaxWatch urged a no vote, warning of a tax shift. Local governments can still raise millage rates, so the savings in my table assume rates hold. I think that's a risk worth watching, not a reason to worry.

What to do now

Look up your own home with the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser's Amendment 3 calculator. If you're moving to Florida, the clock matters: plan to close and become a permanent resident before December 31, 2026, and confirm the details with your tax adviser. If you're thinking about selling, buyers with a deadline are motivated buyers. Either way, I'm happy to sit down and walk through your numbers.
